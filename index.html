Banking Customer Churn

SQL-based EDA on 10,000+ records, feature engineering and K-Means segmentation (6 segments via elbow and silhouette), then a comparison of four classifiers (Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, KNN) against a dummy baseline with 5-fold cross-validation.

I then rebuilt the notebook as a deployable service: a modular Python package managed with uv, a fit/apply preprocessing split saved alongside the model, and a Random Forest served through a FastAPI /predict endpoint. On two test profiles it gave an 11% churn probability for a low-risk customer and 90% for a high-risk one, matching the cluster found earlier. Docker and CI/CD are next.

Honest checks

The Random Forest's AUC of 0.989 looked almost too good, so I retrained it without its two strongest features (transaction amount and count). AUC fell to 0.918 and F1 to 0.664: a real but moderate drop, not a collapse. I also dropped two leftover Naive Bayes columns that came with the source data.
